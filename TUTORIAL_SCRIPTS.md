# Invoice Engine tutorial narration

## THE COMPLETE RETAIL SUITE

Welcome to Invoice Engine, a connected workspace for retail operations. Start with Purchasing to capture supplier invoices, extract key fields, review exceptions, and export clean reports. Billing helps teams build sales bills from a product catalogue and keep each completed transaction recorded. Sales brings the transaction history into clear summaries for revenue, items sold, and performance review. Choose one module or bring the workflows together as your business grows. The aim is less repetitive entry, clearer records, and a smoother daily operation. Explore the separate tours to see each workflow.

## PURCHASING TUTORIAL

Here is the Purchasing workflow. Open the Purchasing workspace and upload a supplier invoice as a supported image or PDF. Invoice Engine runs document reading and AI-assisted field extraction, then normalizes values and checks the result for missing or inconsistent information. Open items flagged for review before relying on them. Use invoice search and filters to find saved records, inspect invoice details, and export the matching results into a formatted combined Excel workbook. Keep the original source documents available for traceability, and verify extracted totals before using reports for accounting decisions.

## BILLING TUTORIAL

The Billing workflow is designed to make checkout consistent. First, maintain a product catalogue with the details your business uses, such as product names, prices, and applicable tax settings. When creating a bill, select products and confirm quantities, prices, and customer details. Review the totals before completing the sale. Save the transaction as the completed sale record, then provide the customer with a receipt or printout where supported. Printing should not create a second sale. Before using Billing for live transactions, confirm that product, tax, inventory, and transaction storage are configured and tested for your deployment.

## SALES TUTORIAL

The Sales workspace is intended to help teams understand what has been sold. Start from completed sales records, then review summaries such as sales totals, transaction history, and items sold where those reports are enabled. Use date ranges and available filters to focus on a period, locate a transaction, or compare activity. Check that refunds, discounts, taxes, and stock movements are represented correctly in your configuration before using the numbers for business decisions. Export or share reports where supported. Clear, consistent records make it easier to spot trends, plan replenishment, and review daily performance.
